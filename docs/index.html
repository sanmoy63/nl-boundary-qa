Boundary data pipeline

Are points assigned to the right vintage of boundary?

Dutch neighbourhood boundaries are re-cut every year. Because a neighbourhood's code is derived from its municipality's, every municipal merger silently renumbers the neighbourhoods inside it. This report measures the consequence.

36.3%

of point observations land in a different neighbourhood depending on which year's map is used — rising to ~45% for records before 2015, and 0% at the reference year, exactly as a self-consistency check requires.

Scope
Demonstrated on 44,184 synthetic points over public CBS boundaries (2015 · 2020 · 2025). The identical pipeline runs privately on NVM housing transactions; those results are reported separately and never published with geometry (GDPR). With three vintages the headline is a deliberate lower bound.

01 — Coverage validation

Is each map a valid coverage?

Six checks per vintage: no broken shapes, no overlaps, no gaps, every neighbourhood nested in its municipality, unique codes, and codes that obey the hierarchy rule. Failures write their geometry to a mappable table; here, there are none.

02 — The measurement

How much does the choice of vintage move the answer?

Each point is assigned twice — once under the map in force in its own year, once under the 2025 reference map — then compared. The bands are the three source maps; the steps fall exactly where the map changed.

2015 map (records 2005–2019) 2020 map (2020–2024) 2025 map — self-check, 0%

03 — The crosswalk

43% of 2015 neighbourhoods changed identity by 2025

For every 2015 neighbourhood, the pipeline finds its 2025 successor by maximum area overlap and classifies what happened — the old-code → new-code map a data customer needs when a new version ships. 12,339 neighbourhoods, matched.

04 — How to trust it

Method & honest limits

Validated by negative control. A test suite that never fails measures nothing. One real neighbourhood was shifted 300 m and the logic re-run: it reported 332,066 m² now outside its municipality and 10 new overlaps — the checks fire on defects that are actually there.

“A check I haven't seen fail is a check I don't trust.”

why a lower bound

With three vintages, a 2007 record is compared against the 2015 map — the nearest one loaded — not a true 2007 map. Real drift is larger. Adding vintages tightens it.

synthetic points

Demo points are spread evenly across land. Real transactions cluster in cities, which is exactly where CBS subdivides most — so on NVM the figure plausibly rises.
